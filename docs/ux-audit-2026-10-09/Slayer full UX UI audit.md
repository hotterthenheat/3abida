# Slayer runs clean but contradicts itself

Slayer is technically healthy and editorially careful, but it often contradicts itself, and that is the main thing to fix. Across 125 addresses, four desk sizes, a phone and both themes there were **no page errors, no failed requests, no horizontal page scroll and no visible banned word**. The faults sit in four places. **Numbers disagree from one surface to the next**: the same stock is $229.90 on the Stocks list and $190.00 one click later, a Compass card says TP1 HIT while its own page says "0 of 3 hit", and the call wall is 480 on one Pinpoint page and 481 on the next. **Some controls quietly do the wrong thing**: an account address with a trailing slash says "You're in", Watchers' and Windows' Expiry filters do nothing, Net Flow plots a made-up ticker, and a reload closes your paper positions without warning. **Keyboard and phone users are second-class**: rows open only with a mouse, overlays do not manage focus, the focus ring is 1 px, there is no skip link, and most terminal controls are under 44 px on a phone. **A few lines break the owner's voice rules**: "the people are invented until the feed lands", a "SAMPLE" badge, a News "Playbook" that says "buy the first pullback", and a "licensed feed" claim. Each of these is fixable in one shared place: one price and level source per name, one confirm-or-undo primitive, one dialog and focus pattern, one New York clock, and one copy sweep. Fixing those few shared pieces clears most of the findings below. Market data is generated locally by design, and the app is UI-only for one user; neither is treated as an issue here.

## How the audit was run and how to read it

Seven audits ran on 2026-10-09 against the Vite dev server at http://localhost:5300 (commit db27023). Each used headless Chromium driven by Playwright at 1440×900, 1280×720, 1920×1080 and 390×844 (plus 3440×1440 and 768×1024 on the landing), in dark and light. They pressed every control they could reach, walked the Tab order, measured font sizes, contrast, clipping and tap targets, scanned all visible text, aria labels, titles, placeholders and alt text for the banned words, and read the source behind each finding. The notes are [landing](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/landing.md), [shell, Pulse and Settings](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/shell_pulse_settings.md), [Compass, Weigher and Terrain](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/compass_weigher_terrain.md), [Pinpoint](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/pinpoint.md), [Trace](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/trace.md), [Dossier and Practice](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/dossier_practice.md) and [outside pages and cross-cutting](../research_notes/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit/outside_crosscutting.md).

The report holds about 435 findings in its tables (16 Broken, 102 Major, 224 Minor, 92 Polish, one suggestion), plus five shorter patterns written as prose (X10–X14). **Screenshot paths** are relative to `research_notes/Slayer full UX UI audit/` (for example `shots-landing/d1440-02-y900.png`). **Code paths** are relative to the repo root unless the file name is unique. **Severity**: Broken (does not work, or shows a false result), Major (misleads, loses work, blocks a common task, or breaks an owner rule in a visible place), Minor (friction, inconsistency, a small access gap), Polish (finish). **"Owner's call"** marks an item that is a product decision rather than a bug. **"Plausible"** marks an item seen only while the machine was heavily loaded (load average 12–70 on 4 cores, with several audits' browsers on the same dev server). Timings from those runs are flagged and not counted as defects.

### X1. Prices, levels and counts disagree between surfaces

This is the most damaging pattern because it costs trust: a trader who sees two numbers for one thing stops believing both. It breaks the owner's rule "Prices agree across desks … never from the config's base price". The causes are a handful of parallel price sources: Dossier reads the static `px` in `src/data/universe.ts:34-36`, Compass's board uses "day-stable lightweight quotes", Pulse tiles read a 10 s scan snapshot beside the live tick, and each Pinpoint page builds its own exposure profile with its own window. **The fix**: one `priceOf(ticker)` and one levels/odds model per name per scan, read by every surface. A figure frozen on purpose (an entry price, a scan time) gets a label: "Entry $0.76 · Now $0.82", "at sweep 10:42:07".

### X2. New York time and the browser's local time are mixed on one screen

The session is New York's (09:30–16:00, and the films and session are set in New York's zone), but many labels are formatted with the browser's zone and no zone suffix. On a UTC or Los Angeles machine this puts trades before the open, windows after the close and a "today" that starts at 07:55. A New York formatter already exists (`src/components/gex/chartTime.ts:31-34`). **The fix**: format every session time with `timeZone: 'America/New_York'`, label it "ET", and have the rail clock read `deskPrefs.clock`.

## What works well and should be kept

The engineering base is unusually clean for a product this dense: **zero page errors, zero failed requests, zero 4xx/5xx and zero phone overflow on 125 routes**, with every legacy address carried to its successor. **The owner's voice rules hold in the rendered UI**: no banned word, no "[placeholder]" and no trade-call verb appeared in any default-state scan, and the trust copy states its limits plainly ("It shows what is there; it doesn't predict what comes next", "We don't offer refunds… a person will look into it"). **The landing's door wiring is perfect** (63 doors, none to a 404), the deal, wall and tour finish within 1.5 s when the machine is idle (even at 4× CPU throttle), the "Rooms" jump lands on the finished wall by 1.2 s, the compare table's sticky header works, and the phone menu, tabs, disclosures and labels are textbook. **Each room has one grammar that carries**: Pinpoint's head, box, scope chip and "How to read" on every box with a plain-English summary under it; Trace's box, facts, champions, cards, sentence and grid across 11 pages; Practice's shared order ticket. **The Practice room's refusals are a model** ("Not enough free money — this needs $116,533.35, and $24,761.35 is free"; "The target has to be above what you pay"). **Terrain's keyboard model is rich and announced** (aria-live), every dropdown in the product closes on Esc and returns focus to its trigger, Weigher's ruler is a real slider, and the expiry calendars have full day labels. **The not-found handling is thoughtful** ("Did you mean Tracker?", `/pricing` → `/#pricing`, both noindex), reduced motion is honoured everywhere but the landing's session, muted text passes AA in both themes, and the light theme follows the owner's one-ground rule in Terrain. Icons, manifest and the share card are complete and correctly sized, and the tab icon follows the mark's state.

## What was not tested, and what that leaves open

Several conclusions here are bounded by what the runs could see. **Films were never played**: headless Chromium cannot decode H.264 (`canPlayType` returned ""), so every landing window fell back to its WebP still. Loop seams, film sharpness and film load timing need a pass in real Chrome or Edge. **No screen reader was used** (NVDA, VoiceOver); every accessibility finding comes from DOM roles, names and Tab walks, so the reading order of the sticky stages, AG Grid announcements and live-tape `aria-live` behaviour are unverified, as are forced colours, Windows High Contrast and 200% zoom. **Safari and iOS were not tested**: svh units, sticky stages and `hidden="until-found"` need checking on real devices, and the "phone" here is Chromium at 390×844, sometimes without touch emulation. **No production build was measured**: all timings come from the Vite dev server (about 6 MB of unbundled JS over 148 requests), so the Pinpoint Map's 10–60 s first paint, News's 6–17 s and the landing's 7.8 s throttled LCP overstate production; the idle long-task cost on the Map is still a real cost. **Timings were taken on a heavily loaded machine** (load average 12–70 on 4 cores, about 95 Chromium processes on one dev server), so every item marked Plausible (slow alerts Esc, phone skeletons after 6 s, the blank phone board, the landing's held-stop faults under load) needs re-measuring on an idle machine; scroll jank was unmeasurable without a GPU. Smaller gaps remain: alert and paper toasts never fired live; Pulse tile drag and resize, Settings import and export, the Pine editor, 33 of 34 drawing tools, Replay, link-letter syncing, the Weigher's Change and Remove, evaluation pass and fail states, debit spreads end to end, tag drag on the chart, the trailing-stop editor, the Compare ruler drag and the News economic calendar were opened or skipped rather than driven through; guides on Ahead, Building and At the wall and the full `TraceGuide.tsx` were sampled, not proof-read; text inside canvases and SVG (axes, chips, footer art) was judged by eye; Inter Display's rendering on Windows, which was the reason it was chosen, is unchecked; and which of the three favicon links Chromium actually uses is unknown. Whether Trace untrack removes or dims a row, and the Compass Tracker's EXPIRED state, could not be produced. None of these gaps undercuts the top fifteen: those were reproduced directly, mostly with the source line that causes them.
