## (a) Which options-data visualizations are strongest today, and which would add most to Slayer's rooms?

### Takeaway
The single most distinctive missing view is a strike × time-of-day heatmap of dealer gamma/charm ("walls through the day", SpotGamma TRACE's core idea), drawn behind the price on Terrain — Slayer already has the strike axis and the per-expiry book but not the intraday time axis. Next most valuable: an IV smile + term-structure pair (a gap in every room today), a probability distribution laid under the Weigher's payoff curve (OptionStrat's pattern), and an OI-change-by-strike bar beside the ladder.

### Cited Findings
- SpotGamma TRACE maps how SPX options positioning may affect intraday moves through three lenses: Gamma, Delta Pressure and Charm Pressure — [SpotGamma support, via search](https://support.spotgamma.com/hc/en-us/articles/33607907909011-What-is-SpotGamma-TRACE); [TRACE user guide](https://spotgamma.com/trace-user-guide)
- In TRACE's gamma heatmap, blue zones are associated with pinning (most strongly near the close) and red zones with volatility; white/black zones mark transitions with little hedging; "price tends to move swiftly through areas of neutral to negative gamma and find support or resistance at areas of strong positive gamma" — [SpotGamma: What is the Gamma Heatmap](https://support.spotgamma.com/hc/en-us/articles/33608037264787-What-is-the-Gamma-Heatmap) (search snippet; the page itself was blocked to fetch)
- Charm Pressure "depicts how options positioning changes with respect to time and can be heavily influenced by large 0DTE volumes"; for market makers it is "a key element in the pinning process … near positive gamma nodes" — [SpotGamma support](https://support.spotgamma.com/hc/en-us/articles/33607907909011-What-is-SpotGamma-TRACE)
- TRACE data updates every 1 minute during the session and offers 5-day forward projections; a companion Strike Plot shows GEX, OI and Net OI by strike — [SpotGamma TRACE User Manual (PDF, 2025)](https://spotgamma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/TRACE-User-Manual-Final-Version.pdf); [SpotGamma support](https://support.spotgamma.com/hc/en-us/articles/33607907909011-What-is-SpotGamma-TRACE)
- QuantWheel offers a free heatmap of modeled vanna and charm by strike and expiration, colour showing sign and intensity — [QuantWheel vanna/charm](https://quantwheel.com/tools/vanna-charm)
- FlashAlpha's API returns VEX and CHEX by strike and labels them with outcomes such as "end-of-day flow prediction" (a framing Slayer must not copy as a call) — [FlashAlpha exposure API](https://flashalpha.com/docs/lab-api-exposure); [FlashAlpha VEX](https://flashalpha.com/docs/lab-api-vex)
- MenthorQ ships its GEX-derived levels as chart lines or a table on TradingView and TrendSpider (API-key paste, 1,300+ assets on TrendSpider); its guide reads heavily positive Net GEX as range-bound/mean-reverting and heavily negative as directional, and pairs Net GEX with Net Delta and term-structure screens — [TrendSpider blog](https://trendspider.com/blog/menthorq-levels-indicators/); [MenthorQ: what is GEX](https://menthorq.com/guide/what-is-gex/)
- OptionStrat's builder plots P/L against underlying price for a chosen day with a date slider; premium users get a probability-distribution overlay of where the stock may sit on that day, and hovering shows the chance of being above/below the hovered price; its optimizer sorts by return or chance of profit — [OptionStrat Options Builder tutorial](https://optionstrat.com/tutorials/options-builder); [OptionStrat profit calculator tutorial](https://optionstrat.com/blog/options-profit-calculator-tutorial)
- OptionCharts upgraded its P/L chart in Sept 2024 (a comparable payoff tool) — [OptionCharts blog](https://optioncharts.io/blog/upgrades-to-profit-loss-chart-september-2024)
- The volatility surface is the 3-D map of implied vol across strikes and expiries; slicing at fixed T gives the smile/skew, at fixed K (usually ATM) the term structure; equity puts usually carry higher IV; term structure normally slopes up and inverts (backwardation) around imminent events or stress — [FlashAlpha volatility surface guide](https://flashalpha.com/articles/what-is-a-volatility-surface-complete-guide); [Wikipedia: Volatility smile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_smile)
- Arbitrage-free SVI is the standard parametrisation for fitting a smooth, arbitrage-free surface from quotes — [Gatheral & Jacquier, arXiv 1204.0646](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.0646)
- Bloomberg lists OVDV among its "Volatility Monitors" (with HVG, VCA, VCUB, NSV, SWIL) in a webinar on the surface's skew, smile and term structure; no public doc describing OVDV's layout was found — [Bloomberg Professional webinar page](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/?p=117442)
- Bookmap's volume dots: each dot is trades at a price level, larger dots for larger size — a proven pattern for showing prints as bubbles on price — [Bookmap learning center](https://bookmap.com/learning-center/getting-started/volume/volume-dots-display)
- lightweight-charts v5 adds multi-pane support and Pane Primitives (plugins attached to panes); custom series and drawing primitives exist since v4.1 — the route to a heatmap behind the candles without a second chart library — [TradingView: Lightweight Charts v5](https://www.tradingview.com/blog/en/tradingview-lightweight-charts-version-5-50837); [v4.1.0 release](https://github.com/tradingview/lightweight-charts/releases/tag/v4.1.0)

## (b) What keeps web trading terminals fast with dense live data, and what would measurably help Slayer?

### Takeaway
Slayer's problem is main-thread churn, not a slow chart renderer: the Pinpoint Map's 53 idle long tasks point at React re-render fan-out on every 1.5 s tick. The measurable wins are (1) batch ticks per animation frame and subscribe components to slices of the store, (2) move exposure/surface computation into a Web Worker, (3) use AG Grid's async transactions for streaming rows, and (4) only then consider OffscreenCanvas or a GPU chart library for the heavy heatmaps.

### Cited Findings
- Each grid data update makes AG Grid redo aggregation, sort and filter and the browser refresh the DOM; `applyTransactionAsync()` holds updates and applies all queued ones in one batch (default 50 ms window, tunable with `asyncTransactionWaitMillis`); recommended for "tens, hundreds or thousands of updates a second" — [AG Grid: High Frequency Updates](https://www.ag-grid.com/react-data-grid/data-update-high-frequency/)
- `scheduler.yield()` splits a long task so the browser can handle input in between, and keeps the continuation at the front of the queue (unlike `setTimeout(0)`); tasks over 50 ms are the target; yielding does not move work off the main thread — CPU-heavy work belongs in a Worker; a production fallback chain is `scheduler.yield()` → `postTask` → `setTimeout`, with only Chromium benefiting from the scheduling APIs — [web.dev PubConsent INP case study](https://web.dev/case-studies/pubconsent-inp); [frontendchecklist: scheduler.yield](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/javascript/scheduler-yield)
- OffscreenCanvas lets canvas drawing run in a Web Worker: `transferControlToOffscreen()` then `postMessage` the canvas as a transferable; a busy main thread does not stall the worker's drawing; available across browsers (MDN: since March 2023; Firefox 105, Safari 16.4) — [web.dev: OffscreenCanvas](https://web.dev/articles/offscreen-canvas); [MDN OffscreenCanvas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/Web/API/OffscreenCanvas)
- LightningChart JS has a beta mode that runs all chart work in a worker and shows it via OffscreenCanvas, best "if you don't need the charting data at all in the main thread"; libraries needing DOM for tooltips/events still keep that part on the main thread — [LightningChart: offscreen canvas guide](https://lightningchart.com/js-charts/docs/more-guides/offscreen-canvas/); [Scott Logic: OffscreenCanvas](https://blog.scottlogic.com/2020/03/19/offscreen-canvas.html)
- Vendor benchmarks (treat as directional): LightningChart reports >60 FPS at 10 channels × 10M points/s and a 30,000 × 30,000 heatmap (900M points) in 5 s; SciChart reports ~40M points/s ingestion in its own suite covering SciChart.js, LightningChart, Plotly, Highcharts, Chart.js, ECharts, uPlot and ChartGPU — [LightningChart performance](https://lightningchart.com/js-charts/performance/); [Arction heatmap comparison repo](https://github.com/Arction/javascript-charts-performance-comparison-heatmaps); [WeAreDevelopers: Inside SciChart](https://platform-v2.wearedevelopers.com/en/companies/4076/scichart/stories/2427)
- lightweight-charts v5: base bundle 35 kB (16% smaller), multi-pane, pane primitives; v5.2.1 improved crosshair/hover performance on charts combining series markers with large datasets and added a drop-in Accessibility plugin — [TradingView v5 blog](https://www.tradingview.com/blog/en/tradingview-lightweight-charts-version-5-50837); [v5.2.1 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/tradingview/lightweight-charts/release/v5.2.1)
- Slayer pins `lightweight-charts ^5.2.0` and ticks with `setInterval(processTick, 1500)` in MarketDataContext; the Pinpoint Map audit measured 53 long tasks / 8.0 s main thread in 20 idle seconds (dev build) and named per-tick rebuilds in ExposureField / TraderClock / WallReportCard and four boxes re-rendering on `marketData` — [Slayer audit, docs/ux-audit-2026-10-09/Slayer full UX UI audit.md, PP-5](file:///home/user/3abida/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-09/Slayer%20full%20UX%20UI%20audit.md)

### Inferences
Ideas for (b), most measurable first. All are UI-only work (no live data needed) and all help more once live data arrives.

1. **Measure on a production build first.** `vite build && vite preview`, Chrome Performance panel + a `PerformanceObserver({type:'long-animation-frame'})` logger on the Map; the audit's numbers are from the dev server with React dev checks. Room: Pinpoint (then all). Effort: **S**. This sets the baseline every other item is judged against.
2. **Stop the context fan-out.** One React context carrying `marketData` re-renders every consumer each tick. Replace with an external store read through `useSyncExternalStore` and per-slice selectors (one symbol's spot, one book's strikes), and coalesce ticks to one publish per `requestAnimationFrame` (or per tick when the tab is visible; pause when `document.hidden`). Precedent: AG Grid's own batching rationale; rAF batching is the standard pattern. Room: global (MarketDataContext), biggest win on Pinpoint Map and Pulse. Effort: **M**. Expected effect: idle long tasks on the Map should fall sharply because ExposureField/TraderClock/WallReportCard stop re-rendering on unrelated ticks — measure with item 1.
3. **Compute in a Web Worker.** Move the exposure surface (`data/exposureSurface.ts`), the 10 s Compass/Pinpoint scans and the seeded history unpacking into a worker; post back typed arrays (transferables). Precedent: web.dev guidance that yielding does not take work off the main thread — Workers do. Room: Pinpoint, Compass, Terrain. Effort: **M** (the engines are already pure and "proven headless", per expectedMovePrimitive.ts' header). When live chains arrive, the same worker parses Polygon/UW payloads.
4. **Yield inside unavoidable main-thread loops.** Wrap first-paint builds (the Map's ledger, Trace's large grids) with `scheduler.yield()` fallback chain so input stays responsive. Effort: **S**.
5. **AG Grid streaming the right way.** Use `getRowId` + `applyTransactionAsync` for Trace's tapes instead of replacing `rowData`; keep cell renderers as plain functions/`valueFormatter` where possible; keep the existing 80-row pagination. Precedent: AG Grid high-frequency docs. Room: Trace, Compass Tracker, Weigher chain. Effort: **S–M**. A canvas grid (e.g. Glide Data Grid) is not justified: AG Grid already virtualizes rows/columns; the audit's grid issues are layout (autoHeight), not render speed.
6. **Heatmaps as one bitmap.** For the strike × time heatmap (section a, idea 1) and the strike × expiry ledger if it grows, draw cells into one `ImageData`/canvas per column inside a lightweight-charts pane primitive; only if it profiles slow, draw in an OffscreenCanvas worker. Effort: **M**. A GPU library (SciChart.js, LightningChart JS — both commercial licences) is overkill for Slayer's point counts (hundreds of strikes × ~400 minutes ≈ 10^5 cells); WebGPU is not needed. Keep lightweight-charts and move to **5.2.1+** for its hover-performance fix and accessibility plugin (**S**).
7. **Virtualize long non-grid lists** (news, alerts, Compass board) with a windowing helper only where lists exceed ~100 rows. Effort: **S**.
8. **Throttle offscreen work.** Use `IntersectionObserver` + `document.visibilityState` to pause charts and timers for panes not on screen (Pulse widgets, Terrain's extra panes); audit the 27 `setInterval`s into one shared clock. Effort: **S**.

### Gaps
- No independent benchmark including lightweight-charts against GPU libraries was found; vendor numbers are self-reported.
- The size of the win from item 2 is not measured — it is an inference from the audit's named suspects and must be checked with item 1.
- `scheduler.yield()` current shipping status in non-Chromium browsers was not confirmed from a primary source.

## (c) How are assistant/LLM features used in trading products in 2025–2026, which are genuinely useful, and how can they stay "a read, never an instruction"?

### Takeaway
The uses that have shipped and stuck are explanations and summaries anchored to sources — "why is this moving" (Robinhood Cortex Digests), earnings-call summaries that link each point to the transcript (Bloomberg), and plain-language Q&A over filings (Fiscal.ai, Public Alpha). For Slayer the best fits are an "explain this level/panel" read built from the terminal's own numbers, a natural-language screen that compiles to the existing Screener's filters, earnings/news summaries in Dossier, and journal reflection in Practice — each constrained to describe and cite, never to suggest a trade.

### Cited Findings
- Robinhood launched Cortex in March 2025, giving Gold members real-time analysis, trade suggestions and stock summaries; its first feature, Digests, explains in plain English why a stock is moving by reading news, analyst reports, technical signals and Robinhood data, shown on the stock page; launched in the UK 19 Aug 2025 after US rollout; Robinhood reports "hundreds of thousands" of users and a self-reported 95% "love" rate — [Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/digests-by-robinhood-cortex-uk); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/08/19/robinhood-launches-stock-picking-ai-summaries/); [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/robinhood-uk-rolls-out-ai-powered-investing-assistant-to-give-customers-trading-confidence/)
- Bloomberg's AI-Powered Earnings Call Summaries (Jan 2024) pull management themes (guidance, supply chain); each summary point links to the matching transcript excerpt and to related Terminal functions; Bloomberg Intelligence analysts helped train the models; the feature works alongside Document Search (NLP over "hundreds of millions" of documents) and reached mobile in July 2024 — [Bloomberg press release](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/bloomberg-launches-ai-powered-earnings-call-summaries); [FintechNews SG](https://fintechnews.sg/84034/ai/bloomberg-introduces-ai-powered-earnings-call-summaries-for-enhanced-financial-analysis/)
- Bloomberg's current AI page also lists ASKB and AI News Summaries over 30,000+ sources — [Bloomberg Professional: AI](https://professional.bloomberg.com/solutions/ai)
- Public's Alpha (2023, GPT-4) is a natural-language research assistant that pulls financials, analyst views and news and summarises earnings calls within about 2 hours; Public states its answers "should not be considered a recommendation" — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2023/05/207353-investing-platform-public-launches-alpha-powered-by-openais-gpt-4-for-investment-research/); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/public-introduces-ai-based-investing-co-pilot-alpha/)
- FinChat became Fiscal.ai: a research terminal with an AI copilot answering in plain language from KPIs, transcripts and segment data, quoting filings directly; free tier 10 prompts/month, Pro reported at $39/month with 250 — affiliate/review sources, unverified — [Toolcenter review 2026](https://www.toolcenter.ai/en/articles/finchat-review-2026); [yespress](https://yespress.io/finchat)
- Community-built MCP servers wrap the Unusual Whales REST API (danwagnerco, phields); no official Unusual Whales MCP server was confirmed — [mcp.so listing](https://mcp.so/servers/mcp-server-unusualwhales); [Glama listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/phields/unusualwhales-mcp/discussions)
- lightweight-charts v5.2.1 ships an "Agent Skill" for AI coding assistants (a sign charting vendors are courting agent workflows) — [v5.2.1 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/tradingview/lightweight-charts/release/v5.2.1)

### Inferences
Ideas for (c). Every assistant output is built from the terminal's own computed numbers (passed as structured context) or from cited text, and passes a wording filter (no buy/sell/enter/exit/target verbs, none of the banned words). All need an LLM backend, which Slayer does not have yet — so "UI-only now" means the UI and a deterministic template version can be built now, with the model swapped in later.

1. **"Read this" on any panel or level.** A small button on a wall, the flip, a heatmap cell, a Compass card: returns 2–4 sentences describing what the numbers are and how they changed ("475 has carried the largest call gamma since 11:20; it grew 18% in the last hour; price is 0.4% below it"), with each figure linked to the panel it came from. Precedent: Robinhood Digests ("why it's moving"), Bloomberg's linked summary points. Room: **Terrain, Pinpoint, Compass**. UI-only now: **yes, as templates** (Slayer already writes reading sentences — e.g. the session's `readings` and BookRead.tsx); the LLM later only smooths phrasing. Effort: **S** (templates) / **M** (LLM).
2. **Command-bar assistant.** Extend `CommandPalette.tsx` (today: Navigate / Ticker / Draw) with an "Ask" group that turns a sentence into an action the terminal already supports — open a page, set a ticker, apply a screen, set an alert ("alert me if SPY crosses the flip"). Precedent: Bloomberg Document Search, Public "just swipe down and ask". Room: global shell. UI-only now: a rule-based parser is possible now (**M**); LLM function-calling later (**M**). Never "should I…" answers: questions asking for a decision get the read instead.
3. **Natural-language screening.** "0DTE calls over $1M premium on tech names above the call wall" compiles to Trace Screener / Compass filters shown as editable chips before running — the user sees and owns the filter. Precedent: Public Alpha screens; Fiscal.ai plain-language questions. Room: **Trace Screener, Compass**. Needs live data to be meaningful; UI can be built now. Effort: **M**.
4. **Earnings-call and news summaries with citations.** Each bullet links to the transcript line or article; earnings day shows the implied move vs the realised move. Precedent: Bloomberg (Jan 2024), Public Alpha (~2 h after a call), Fiscal.ai. Room: **Dossier**. Needs live data (transcripts/news API). Effort: **M**. Also clear the existing wording issue the audit flagged (News "buy the first pullback", X8).
5. **Journal reflection.** After a session, a written read of the user's own paper trades: entries relative to walls/flip/expected move, hold times, what changed while holding, patterns across weeks — descriptive, from the journal's data. Precedent: no direct public precedent found (see Gaps); it is the safest LLM use because it reads the user's own record. Room: **Practice** (Journal). UI-only now: **yes** — paper trades and MAE/MFE are already stored. Effort: **M**.
6. **MCP server for Slayer itself (later).** Expose the terminal's readings (walls, flip, flow summaries) as MCP tools so the owner's own assistant can query them. Precedent: community Unusual Whales MCP servers. Needs a backend. Effort: **L**.

Keeping it "a read, never an instruction": (i) the model receives only computed facts + citations and must quote figures from them (no free recall of prices); (ii) a system prompt and a post-filter that rejects imperative trading verbs and the banned words (grade, score, win rate, signal-as-call, guaranteed, confluence, market intelligence); (iii) every sentence carries a source chip (panel, transcript line); (iv) questions asking "should I buy/sell" are answered with the current read and a plain statement that Slayer does not make calls (Public's "not a recommendation" stance is the precedent). Note Robinhood Cortex explicitly includes "trade suggestions" — the counter-example Slayer should not follow.

- Public Alpha sources are 2023–2024; current options capabilities unknown.
- No usage/retention data for any of these features except Robinhood's self-reported survey.

## Sound and accessibility: audio cues, colour-blind-safe palettes, keyboard-first operation

### Takeaway
Slayer already has Web-Audio sounds and a colour-blind note on its heatmap ramps; the gaps are an optional tape "pulse" sound, a colour-vision setting for bull/bear that also changes shape (not only hue), and keyboard row-opening plus focus fixes the audit already lists (X4, X6, X13). These are cheap, UI-only and differentiating.

### Cited Findings
- Bookmap volume dots encode trade size as dot size; Bookmap's alert sounds are fixed and not customisable (2019 backlog, 2024 request); a secondhand source describes "Market Pulse", an audio feed of buying and selling pressure with adjustable thresholds and half-life — [Bookmap volume dots](https://bookmap.com/learning-center/getting-started/volume/volume-dots-display); [Bookmap forum: custom voice request](https://bookmap.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8825); [Gist summary of Bookmap webinar (secondhand)](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/mastering-bookmap-live-trading-webinar-insights)
- Okabe-Ito / Wong palettes are the most cited colour-blind-safe sets (blue #0072B2, orange #E69F00, vermillion #D55E00); IBM's offers #648FFF / #FE6100; red-green deficiency is the most common, and NCEAS advises blue/red or blue/orange if red-green must be avoided — [NCEAS colour-blind safe schemes (PDF)](https://nceas.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Colorblind%20Safe%20Color%20Schemes.pdf); [AudioEye](https://www.audioeye.com/post/colorblind-friendly-palettes/)
- Futu's app offers a pink-green scheme for red-green colour-blind users and pairs it with hollow vs solid candles so direction does not depend on colour alone; other screens use distinguishable blue/orange — [Android Developers: Futubull story](https://developer.android.com/stories/apps/futu)
- lightweight-charts 5.2.1 adds a drop-in Accessibility plugin — [v5.2.1 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/tradingview/lightweight-charts/release/v5.2.1)
- Slayer's audit lists keyboard and accessibility gaps: rows open by mouse only (X6), focus not taken/held by search, alerts drawer, cards and guides (X13), 1 px focus ring, no skip link, no `<main>`, missing h1s (X4) — [Slayer audit WHAT-REMAINS.md](file:///home/user/3abida/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-09/WHAT-REMAINS.md)

### Inferences
1. **Colour-vision setting for direction.** Settings › Appearance: "Standard / Blue–orange / High contrast", swapping `--bull`/`--bear` tokens (theme/tokens.css already centralises them) and adding a non-colour cue — hollow vs filled candles, ▲/▼ on figures, outlined vs filled flow bubbles. Precedent: Futu, Okabe-Ito. Room: global. UI-only: yes. Effort: **S**. Must keep the paper-theme contrast rules (direction inks ~4.3:1).
2. **Tape pulse sound (opt-in).** A quiet Web-Audio tick per large print, pitch by side, rate-limited, plus a distinct tone when price crosses a wall or the flip — added to `core/sound.ts` with its own Settings switch. Precedent: Bookmap Market Pulse (secondhand), DAS-style print sounds (not verified here). Room: **Trace Live Tape**, **Terrain**. UI-only: yes. Effort: **S**. Respects the rule that a page never plays before a gesture and the landing stays silent.
3. **Keyboard-first operation.** j/k or ↑/↓ + Enter to open rows across Trace/Pinpoint/Compass (X6), `/` to search, `g t` / `g p` style room jumps, `?` for a shortcut sheet, and the lightweight-charts accessibility plugin on charts. Precedent: terminal convention (Bloomberg-style keyboard use) — not separately sourced here. Room: global. UI-only: yes. Effort: **M** (spread across rooms). Fixing X4/X6/X13 is the base.
